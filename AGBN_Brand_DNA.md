# AGBN Brand DNA
**Africa & Global Business Network**
Prepared by CloudIA. This document is the single source of truth for how AGBN looks, sounds, and feels across the new website and any future material. It exists so that every future decision, a new page, a new blog post, a new ad, can be checked against something concrete instead of "does this feel right."

This document is built from AGBN's existing identity (mission, membership tiers, sector focus, tone already present in their current site and messaging) and sharpened into a system that is distinctive and buildable, not a generic business-network template.

---

## 1. Brand Essence

**Mission**
To build Africa's largest opportunity-sharing ecosystem, where connections become partnerships and opportunities become income.

**Vision**
A continent where every entrepreneur has access to opportunities, markets, and meaningful business relationships, regardless of which of the 54 countries they start in.

**What AGBN actually does, in one sentence**
AGBN turns a business owner's existing network into a source of income, by connecting people to vetted opportunities across sectors and paying commission when referrals become deals.

**The one thing to never lose sight of**
AGBN is not a content platform or a social network. It is an economic engine. Every design and copy decision should make the site feel like a place where real money and real deals move, not a brochure.

---

## 2. Brand Values

| Value | What it means on the site |
|---|---|
| **Trust first** | Every claim is backed by something visible. No stat without a number, no promise without a way to verify it. This is the value the current site violates most, and the one the rebuild has to protect hardest. |
| **Pan-African, not one-country** | Never let the design or copy default to a single country's visual cues. Sector categories, member stories, and case studies should visibly span the continent. |
| **Opportunity is specific, not abstract** | Avoid vague "grow your business" language where a real sector, number, or outcome can be used instead. |
| **Access over exclusivity** | The tone is ambitious but not gatekeeping. Elite tier language ("Be seen. Be trusted. Be preferred.") should read as aspirational, not intimidating to a first-time visitor. |

---

## 3. Who We're Talking To

**Primary: The Connector.** Runs an established SME or mid-size business, 30 to 55 years old, already has a strong personal or industry network, and is used to referring business informally without ever being paid for it. Wants to know: "What's actually in this for me, and how do I know it's legitimate?"

**Secondary: The Opportunity Seeker.** Younger entrepreneur or professional, growing a business or looking to expand into new markets or countries. Wants to know: "Is there something here for a business my size, right now?"

**Tertiary: The Strategic Partner.** Investor, corporate, or institution (an "Africa Elite" tier prospect) evaluating whether AGBN is a credible, well-run platform worth being associated with. Wants to know: "Does this look like an organisation I'd put my name next to?"

All three personas share one filter: they will decide whether AGBN is real within the first ten seconds on the site. Every above-the-fold decision should be made with that filter in mind.

---

## 4. Voice and Tone

AGBN's voice is **confident, direct, and grounded**. It talks like a serious business network, not a startup and not a nonprofit. It never oversells, because the audience is business people who can smell a pitch.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Connect with business owners, discover opportunities, refer deals, earn commissions." | "Unlock a revolutionary new way to network." |
| "700 members across 7 countries." | "Join thousands of entrepreneurs" (only if the number is real and current) |
| "Registration for this event isn't open yet. We'll email you the moment it is." | "RSVP Currently Closed. Please check back later." |
| Short, declarative sentences. | Long compound sentences stacking three benefits into one line. |
| Naming the sector or outcome specifically ("mining and manganese opportunities in Zambia"). | Generic categories with no specificity ("exciting opportunities await"). |

**The four words that should show up everywhere, because they are the actual product**: Connect. Refer. Earn. Grow. Keep this sequence intact. It is AGBN's version of a value proposition and it already works, it should not be reworded per page.

**Tagline**
"Turn your network into income." This is the strongest line already in use. Keep it as the primary tagline across the site and marketing.

**Secondary lines already worth preserving**
- "Africa is bigger together."
- "Building connections. Awarding referrals."
- "One network. 54 countries. Endless opportunities."

## 5. Visual Identity

### 5.1 Why this system, not a template

The current site's navy-and-gold combination is the right instinct. Deep blue reads as institutional and trustworthy; gold reads as value and opportunity, both things AGBN needs to signal immediately to a first-time visitor deciding whether to pay to join. The problem in the current build isn't the palette, it's that it was never turned into a disciplined system: two different card systems, an unplanned green and orange showing up only on one page, and text that disappears against its own background.

The rebuild keeps navy and gold as the core, formalises the palette so nothing "accidental" ever ships again, and adds one signature visual device tied directly to what AGBN does: a network, not a generic business template.

### 5.2 Colour System

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep Navy** (Primary) | `#0A1D37` | Header, footer, primary backgrounds, primary text on light surfaces |
| **Midnight** (Primary, darker) | `#061426` | Hero backgrounds, high-contrast sections, dark-mode surfaces |
| **Signal Gold** (Accent, primary CTA) | `#CCA43B` | Primary buttons, key numbers, active nav state, price figures |
| **Ember Gold** (Accent, hover/active state) | `#E5B945` | Hover and pressed states only, never a resting state |
| **Savanna Green** (Secondary, growth/success) | `#1F7A4D` | Success states, confirmation messages, "Grow" tier accents, positive stats |
| **Clay** (Secondary, energy/warmth) | `#B85C2E` | Sparingly: sector tags, editorial highlights in blog/case study content. Never used as a primary CTA colour. |
| **Slate 900** (Text) | `#101820` | Body text on light backgrounds |
| **Slate 500** (Muted text) | `#5B6B7A` | Captions, metadata, secondary text |
| **Slate 200** (Borders/dividers) | `#DCE3EA` | Card borders, table lines |
| **Slate 50** (Surface) | `#F6F8FA` | Page background on light sections |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | Card surfaces, reversed text on dark backgrounds |

**Rules that must never be broken again**
- Every colour used anywhere on the site must come from this table. No page gets to introduce its own palette, ever, including future blog posts or case studies added through the CMS.
- Gold text is never placed on a light or white background. Gold only sits on Navy, Midnight, or as a border/underline on light backgrounds. This single rule is what fixes the invisible "Download App" button from the current site.
- One primary action per screen gets Signal Gold. If a screen has three buttons, only the most important one is gold; the rest are outline or ghost style.

### 5.3 Typography

The current site uses the operating system's default font. That is invisible, forgettable, and free to use, which is exactly the problem: it makes AGBN look like a template, and a fintech-adjacent, trust-dependent brand cannot look like a template.

| Role | Typeface | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Display** (H1, hero headlines, pricing figures) | **Clash Display** (Fontshare, free, self-hostable) | Confident, geometric, slightly architectural. Reads as modern and international without tipping into "generic startup sans." |
| **Body** (paragraphs, nav, buttons, forms) | **General Sans** (Fontshare, free, self-hostable) | Clean, highly legible at small sizes, pairs naturally with Clash Display without fighting it. |
| **Data / Utility** (stats, prices, dates, tags) | **Azeret Mono** (Google Fonts, open source) | Gives numbers, prices, and stats a precise, engineered feel, reinforcing "real numbers, not marketing fluff." Its numerals are flat-sided and distinctive, so figures read as deliberate rather than defaulted. |

*Amended 3 September 2026.* This role originally specified IBM Plex Mono. Plex does the job competently but has become the default mono of developer tooling and AI-generated layouts, which makes it read as absent rather than chosen — the same "looks like a template" failure §5.3 exists to prevent. Azeret Mono was selected against this site's actual mono usage, which is almost entirely uppercase kickers at 12px/0.14em tracking and figures at Display M/L: it stays narrow enough that tracked captions don't sprawl (ruling out Martian Mono) and ships the 400/500/600 weights in use (ruling out Fragment Mono).

**Type scale (base 16px)**: Display XL 64px / Display L 40px / Display M 28px / Body L 18px / Body M 16px / Body S 14px / Caption 12px. Line height 1.5 for body, 1.1 to 1.2 for display sizes.

### 5.4 The Signature Element: The Network Map

Every distinctive brand needs one visual idea that could not be copy-pasted onto a competitor's site. For AGBN, that is **the network map**: a thin-line, node-and-connection graphic where dots (members, businesses, opportunities) connect to each other with fine gold lines, occasionally tracing the rough silhouette of the African continent.

This is not decoration. It is a literal picture of the product: a network of people connecting to become income. Use it deliberately and sparingly:

- **Homepage hero**: the node-and-connection motif, full-bleed across the band, with the continent implied by the density and arrangement of gold points rather than drawn as a filled shape or outlined with borders. The copy sits in the artwork's deliberately empty left two-thirds.

  **Amended 3 September 2026.** This slot briefly carried a documentary photograph instead. That is reverted: the motif is the hero anchor, as this section always intended.

  The motif is layered over a dusk photograph of the Sandton skyline, held well back by a navy wash so it reads as depth rather than as a competing subject. This is the one place on the site where §5.5's "real photography" and the §5.4 motif share a frame; the photograph is texture, and the motif stays the subject.

  Three implementation notes that matter if it is ever regenerated:
  - The artwork sits over the photograph using `mix-blend-screen`, which discards black and leaves only the gold. The shipped asset (`hero-network-africa.webp`) has been processed to a **true black** background and cropped tight to the continent; the raw generation's Midnight background was close, but not close enough — see the next note.
  - The artwork is sized with `object-contain`, not `object-cover`. Under `cover`, how much of the continent survived depended on the window's aspect ratio: at 1920x711 both the top of North Africa and the bottom of South Africa were cropped away. `contain` guarantees the whole continent fits at any viewport, and it is only invisible because the background is true black — against Midnight the letterboxing would screen as a lighter rectangle over the photograph. Regenerated artwork must be put through the same black-background processing or both properties break together.
  - The artwork is a raster image (`public/home/hero-network-africa.jpg`), not the animated `NetworkMap` SVG, so the "lines draw themselves" behaviour described above is **not** currently present in the hero. The `hero` variant of the `NetworkMap` component is unused; it is kept rather than deleted so the animated treatment can be restored without rebuilding it.
  - The empty left two-thirds is load-bearing, not decoration. It is what lets the headline sit on real negative space instead of over detail, and it is why the hero needs only a light scrim. Any replacement artwork has to preserve it.

  The restraint rule below still holds: the motif appears at most once or twice per page. It appears twice on the homepage — here, and as the divider above the final call to action.
- **Loading states**: a single node "pulsing" while content loads, instead of a generic spinner.
- **Section dividers**: a thin gold line-and-node motif instead of a plain horizontal rule.
- **Empty states** (e.g. no opportunities yet in a filtered category): an unconnected node, with copy that invites action rather than apologises.

Do not use this motif as a repeated background pattern or wallpaper. Its power is in restraint, it appears at most once or twice per page.

### 5.5 Imagery and Photography

- Real photography only, no generic stock business handshakes. Sector imagery (mining, agriculture, construction, etc) should look specifically African: real sites, real people, real equipment, not a generic Getty Images business montage.

**Interim status, 3 September 2026.** The seed imagery currently in the build is AI-generated and does **not** satisfy the rule above. It replaced navy gradient placeholders stamped "AGBN — PLACEHOLDER IMAGE", so it is a large improvement, but generated documentary imagery is not photography and must not be allowed to quietly become "the photography." Real commissioned photography is planned for the gallery section; replace these as it arrives. Provenance for every generated image — subject, country, prompt discipline, and a `generated` flag — is recorded in `src/seed/assets/manifest.json`.

Two constraints learned while producing that set, worth keeping for any future generated asset:
- The image model has no negative conditioning. Writing "no handshake" into a prompt **produced** a handshake — the exact composition this section forbids. Never phrase a constraint as an exclusion; positively describe what is in frame instead.
- Give every person in a frame a distinct, physically separate task. That is what structurally prevents handshake and posed-group compositions, not asking for their absence.
- One country per image, never repeated across a set. This enforces §2's "pan-African, not one-country" rule in the asset layer rather than trusting copy to carry it.
- Member and case study photography should be honest portraits, not staged corporate headshot walls.
- Every image that is decorative rather than informative should be compressed and served responsively (see build instructions), this was one of the biggest failures of the current site.

### 5.6 Logo Usage

- Primary lockup: circular emblem (African continent motif) + wordmark, gold on navy.
- Minimum clear space around the logo equals the height of the emblem itself.
- Never place the gold wordmark on a light background. On light backgrounds, use the navy wordmark version.
- One consistent lockup across every page. The current site's inconsistency (different logo text treatment on the About page versus every other page) must not happen again; this is a CMS global setting, not something a page template can override.

### 5.7 Motion Principles

- Motion should always mean something: a connection forming, content loading, a state changing. Never decorative bouncing or spinning for its own sake.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences everywhere, this is both an accessibility requirement and a trust signal.
- One orchestrated moment per page beats five small scattered animations. The homepage hero's network map drawing itself is the one big moment; everything else on the page should be calm.

## 6. Content Pillars (for Blog and Case Studies)

The CMS-backed blog and case study sections should not be a generic "news" feed. Organise all future content around four pillars so the content strategy has a spine from day one:

1. **Deal Stories** — real, specific stories of referrals that became income (with real numbers where the member allows it). This is the single most persuasive content type AGBN can produce, and should be prioritised over generic thought-leadership posts.
2. **Sector Spotlights** — a deep look at one sector at a time (mining this month, green energy next), matching the ten sector categories already on the site.
3. **Country Spotlights** — reinforcing the pan-African, 54-country promise with real, specific coverage rather than treating "Africa" as one market.
4. **Network Notes** — practical advice on referring well, building trust across borders, and getting the most from membership. This is where AGBN teaches its own product.

Every blog post and case study should carry a sector tag and a country tag at minimum, these become filterable fields in the CMS and directly power a future "browse by sector/country" experience.

---

## 7. What This Document Protects Against

This brand system exists specifically to prevent the failures found in the current site's audit:
- A page built in isolation with its own colours and fonts (the About page problem).
- Text that is technically present but functionally invisible (the Download App button problem).
- A stat or number shipped without real data behind it ("Commission Earned" with no figure).
- Generic, could-be-anyone copy that doesn't reflect AGBN's specific sectors, countries, or numbers.

Any future contributor, human or AI, should be able to read this document and immediately know whether a proposed design or piece of copy belongs on the AGBN site.
